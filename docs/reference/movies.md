# Movies

The game's movies are AV1 in WebM. The SDK's movie library in `subsdk0` demuxes them natively and decodes through `nn::codec`, which the host takes over in `dm/host/hle_codec_video.c` and decodes in `dm/host/hle_movie_av1.c` (VideoToolbox where the Mac has a hardware AV1 decoder, dav1d elsewhere); `dm/host/hle_movie.c` holds the signal that tells the frame step a movie is playing. Each entry: symptom, cause, fix, where it was found. The [symptom index](../agents/symptoms.md) lists every symptom.

## The Switch 2's decoder API

### `nn::codec` AV1 decoder taken over

- **Symptom:** a Switch 2 game crashes when a movie starts: `nn::codec::video::Initialize` faults in the SDK, and before that `nv::InitCheck` aborts ([`hle_nv.c`](../../dm/gfx/hle_nv.c)). Later, when a second movie starts: `game called nn::diag abort: Result 0` in `nn::os::ClearSystemEvent` or `TryWaitSystemEvent` on the decoder's event.
- **Cause:** the Switch 2's SDK decodes video through `nn::codec` (H.264, AV1, VP8 and VP9 decoders over a decoder service, decoder image pools, NV12 / RGBA readback); every call goes to a service the host does not serve.
- **Fix:** `dm/host/hle_codec_video.c` takes the API over for AV1, the codec of each of Ocarina of Time's 26 movies (`Event/Movie/*.webm`, `V_AV1`, no sound track): the decoder image pools, the AV1 decoder's entry points by their SDK 22 names, the readback calls and the sync point events. `hle_movie_av1.c` decodes ([AV1 movies through SDK 22's decoder](#av1-movies-through-sdk-22s-decoder)). The protocol as the SDK implements it: one image pool per output picture, registered with the decoder under an index; registering gives the buffer to the decoder to fill and puts the index in an 8-deep queue of acquirable buffers, with a sync handle that is signalled when a picture is in it ([below](#registered-decoder-buffers-signalled-before-a-picture-was-decoded)). `Release…OutputBuffer` gives the buffer back to the decoder to fill and puts it back in the queue at once, with a new sync handle that is signalled when the next picture is there. Decoded pictures are kept as NV12 (`cv_pic`: tight rows, the matrix and range of the stream), which is what the game reads (`ReadDecoderOutputImageNv12`) and what both decoders produce; `ReadDecoderOutputImageRgba` converts.
- **The other codecs are refused:** the movie library in `subsdk0` imports the VP9, VP8 and H.264 decoders too and never calls them. Their `Initialize…ByTransferMemory` / `ByRemoteMemory` calls are taken over to print `[RED] nn::codec: a <codec> decoder was asked for: this host decodes AV1 only` and fail, so a movie of another codec reaches the library's error path, not a service that is not there. The other entry points of those decoders are not taken over: without an initialized decoder the library does not call them.
- **Check:** `make -C dm/host/tests run` runs `t_codec_video` (AddressSanitizer and UBSan): the buffer queue and its results, sync handles and sync point events, NV12 / RGBA readback of a 64x64 AV1 key frame with cropped and padded read sizes, packets refused while 12 pictures and packets wait, end of stream, flush, a pool finalized while its buffer is still registered, and a decoder finalized and made again in the same cleared memory. In game: a headless run of the title (its movie plays behind the menu) prints `[movie] decoder …: 60 pictures a second` and, at Finalize, the packet times.
- **Found on:** [The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/README.md): on an M2 Pro (dav1d), 125 s of the title and a save load: 2,373 packets decoded in 4.11 ms each, 94.61 ms the longest, 7 pictures after their buffer was released, 60.0 fps, no red line (2026-11-21). The first host decoder for this API was written for Donkey Kong Bananza's VP9 guide movies.
- **Open:** encrypted input, alias pools and the readback of part of a picture are not served (each prints a red line once). The VideoToolbox path (M3 and later) was not run after the pictures became NV12: this Mac has no hardware AV1 decoder, and `t_codec_video` runs its VideoToolbox cases only where one exists.

### Registered decoder buffers signalled before a picture was decoded

- **Symptom:** `game called nn::diag abort` with Result 0x1c286f (nn::codec 3604) on `VideoDecoderThread` when a movie starts, now and then: in nxnative about 1 time in 5 after a race in Mario Kart World 1.0.0. Not seen in this game's logs.
- **Cause:** `hle_codec_video.c` registered output buffers with generation 0 and left them out of the decoder's pending-output queue, and `sync_reached` took any generation-0 handle as signalled. In the Switch 2 `sdk` (checked in 20.5.19 and 21.4.0), `Register…DecoderBuffer` (IPC `0x147`) has the client-side protocol of `Release…DecoderOutputBuffer` (IPC `0x14a`): both hand the buffer to `VideoDecService`, keep the `NativeSyncHandle` it returns and queue the index. `NintendoSDK_movie`'s `VideoDecoder::RegisterVideoFrame` (this game's `subsdk0+0x3a40`, SDK 22.2.8; the same in SDK 19 to 21) calls `Register…DecoderBuffer`, `Acquire…DecoderOutputBuffer` and `RequestSyncPointEvent`, with no `Release`, and returns so the game can call `Start`. With generation 0 signalled at once, arming the event fired it at once; playback held only when the worker took that signal before `Start` and recycled the buffer through its stopped branch. When `Start` came first, the worker read the empty buffer's properties: 3604.
- **Fix:** `cv_dec_reg` moves `buf[i].gen` on (from 1), queues the registered buffer in `pend[]` as well as `fifo[]`, and fills and signals as `cv_dec_rel` does; `sync_reached` has no case for generation 0, so a registered buffer's handle signals only once a picture (or the end of stream) is in it. `cv_dec_rel` takes the index out of `pend[]` before queueing it again (`cv_drop_index`).
- **Check:** `t_codec_video` (ASan and UBSan): register, acquire, `RequestSyncPointEvent` and `Start` leave the handle and the event unsignalled until a submission decodes a picture into the registered buffer, with no release; a buffer registered while pictures wait takes one at once; a release of an unfilled buffer does not queue it twice.
- **Found on:** Mario Kart World 1.0.0, in nxnative (upstream `a41f0270`, [PR #3173](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/3173)); the protocol read by disassembly there in Donkey Kong Bananza (SDK 20.5.19), Mario Kart World 1.8.0 (SDK 21.4.0), [The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/README.md) (SDK 22.2.8) and Fire Emblem Fortune's Weave.

### AV1 movies through SDK 22's decoder

- **Symptom:** a crash in `nn::codec::video::InitializeAv1DecoderByTransferMemory` when a movie starts.
- **Cause:** SDK 22 initializes its decoders by transfer or remote memory, with stack arguments, and the game's movies are AV1 decoded into native images: linear textures it makes over the output pool's memory with `nvnNativeImage*` and `nvnTextureInitializeFromNativeImage`.
- **Fix:** `hle_codec_video.c` takes the SDK 22 forms and the native images (16 KB aligned, 64-byte stride); `hle_movie_av1.c` decodes on VideoToolbox, building the av1C record from the stream's sequence header. VideoToolbox's AV1 decoder is hardware only (M3 and A17 Pro and later). Elsewhere, for a stream VideoToolbox refuses, or with `DM_AV1_DAV1D=1`, dav1d decodes ([`dm/third_party/dav1d`](../../dm/third_party/dav1d/README.md), 1.5.4 with its arm64 assembly, built by `dm/host/Makefile` into `gen/dav1d/libdav1d.a`). Its planar pictures are handed over as NV12, 10- and 12-bit streams shifted to 8 bits. A stream with no colour matrix is taken as BT.601 below 720 lines and BT.709 from there, as VideoToolbox does. Pictures cannot be left black instead: the game's movie module aborts on a decoder that does not initialize (`movie::detail::InitializeDecoderByTransferMemory`, `subsdk2+0xf9b4` in Fire Emblem Fortune's Weave).
- **Check:** `make -C dm/host/tests GAME=<id> run` runs `t_codec_video` (AddressSanitizer and UBSan), which covers the AV1 and SDK 22 forms, native images and the av1C record, and decodes a 64x64 AV1 key frame (SVT-AV1) on each backend the Mac has, read back as NV12.
- **Found on:** Fire Emblem Fortune's Weave: 82 movies in WebM at 2560x1440; the opening's 3,301 pictures play. On #957's branch, VideoToolbox and dav1d gave the same pictures, bit for bit, over the opening's first 600 (about 450 pictures a second each on an M3 Pro), and a tester's M1 Pro played the first two movies through dav1d, smooth and in sync ([seedds/nxnative#927](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/927)).

### The submission-ready event counted as packets

- **Symptom:** a movie stays black with the game waiting behind it, the decoder made and no `first picture` line (`Submit…InputBuffer` never called). Or `[RED] nn::codec VP9: Corrupt frame detected`, then `Keyframe / intra-only frame required`, each time about 6 s into a movie or after skipping in one.
- **Cause:** Mario Tennis Fever's player sends one packet per signal of the event it counts, and ignores `DecodeAsync`'s result (`nro0+0xd16130`), so each signal has to mean room for a packet. The host signalled at `Initialize`, which the player clears and drops while it is not started, and then only at releases. So nothing came after `Start`, and it never sent a packet. With a signal at `Start`, it then got one per release while a seek held back the samples, its count grew, and the host's refusal of a packet with 4 pictures waiting lost a frame for good: libvpx found everything after it corrupt until the next key frame.
- **Fix:** `Start…Decoder` signals (`cv_dec_start`). After that the event rises when a packet was taken or room came back, not at every release (`ready`), and goes down at 4 waiting, while packets are refused only at 12 (`CV_READY`, `CV_HOLD`), so a signal in flight still finds room. What waits is counted the same whether a packet has been decoded yet or not: the pictures without a released buffer, the packets queued and the one in decode (`cv_pending`).
- **A refusal is no fault:** the refusal returns nn::codec 3603, which `NintendoSDK_movie` expects: `detail::SubmitDecoderInputBuffer` turns exactly that result into its own retry code 0x7a, which `VideoDecoder::DecodeAsync` hands back to its player, and aborts on any other failure (Ocarina of Time's `subsdk0` +0x10fd20, +0x103840). It is logged as `nn::codec <codec>: busy (result 3603), a packet refused …`, no longer as a red line (Ocarina of Time AV1, Kirby Air Riders VP9: 4 a run, movies play). The host drops no picture: the decoder's thread waits for room before it holds another (`cv_room`).
- **Check:** `t_codec_video`.
- **Found on:** Mario Tennis Fever, every movie.

### `nn::codec` packets decoded inside the submission

- **Symptom:** a movie stutters in small regular jerks (a picture stays for two or three frames, then the movie jumps on) while the frame rate reads a steady 60 and the frame times are flat: the game's frames are on time, the movie's pictures are not. Worst where the host decodes in software.
- **Cause:** the SDK's `Submit…InputBuffer` hands the packet to the decoder service and returns; the host decoded the packet inside the call, on the caller. A player that does all of a movie's work on one thread loses that thread for the length of each decode. Ocarina of Time's (in `main`) runs `DecodeAsync`, `AcquireVideoFrame` and `ReleaseVideoFrame` on its reader thread, and gives the shown picture's buffer back from there; its frame loop's `advance` (`main+0x3b61ea0`) keeps the picture on screen when no decoded frame is ready or the shown one has not been given back yet (the two failures at `main+0x3b61ed8`, `x8` zero or not). On an M2 Pro with dav1d, `DecodeAsync` took 1.9 ms at the median, 31 ms at the 90th percentile and 120 ms at the 99th, and `advance` failed in 546 and 569 of about 2,900 frames of the title (two runs; three in four of them or more with the picture not given back). The frame loop never waited, so the frame times showed nothing.
- **Fix:** each decoder decodes on a thread of its own (`cv_worker` in `hle_codec_video.c`). A submission copies its packet into the decoder's queue and returns (`cv_dec_submit`); the queue, the pictures waiting for a released buffer and the packet in decode count together for the submission-ready event and the refusal ([the event counted as packets](#the-submission-ready-event-counted-as-packets)), so a client sees the same event whatever the decoder's speed. The decoder's thread puts a picture into a released buffer and signals that buffer's sync point event itself (`hle_signal_system_event` is the host's, callable from any thread); with 12 pictures held it waits for a release before it decodes on. `Stop` and `Flush` drop the queued packets and the held pictures at once and number the stream anew, so the pictures of a packet still in decode are dropped when it ends; the codec is reset by its own thread before the next packet. Finalize joins the thread. Every guest call takes the decoder's lock as a host lock (`dm_host_mutex_lock`).
- **After:** `DecodeAsync` 0.10 / 0.20 / 1.1 ms, and 5 to 7 failed `advance` calls in about 2,900 frames with dav1d as it was; 0 or 1 with [dav1d on frame threads](#dav1d-decoded-one-frame-at-a-time).
- **Check:** `t_codec_video`: the event and the refusal are the same with the packets decoded or not, a submission signals its event itself and a sync point event comes from the decoder's thread, eight flushes with six undecoded packets each give only the key frame sent after them, and a decoder is finalized with six packets undecoded.
- **Found on:** [The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/README.md), its title movie (AV1, 1920x1080 at 60 fps) on an M2 Pro, headless, with `DM_PROBE=main:3b61ed8,main:3b61f80` counting the failed and the good `advance` calls. Again on an M4 Pro (48 GB, macOS 27.2) with dav1d forced (`DM_AV1_DAV1D=1`), 54 s of the title, one run a side: 361 failed of 3,041 before, 0 of 3,057 with both fixes, the frame times and red lines the same. Mario Kart World's cup opening movie (VP9, 2560x1440, libvpx) on the same Mac: all 1,423 pictures before and after, frame p95 18.8 to 19.3 ms in the movie on both, 3.24 ms a packet and 13.4 ms the longest, 2 pictures after their buffer.

### dav1d decoded one frame at a time

- **Symptom:** on a chip without a hardware AV1 decoder (M1, M2), a 1080p 60 fps AV1 movie shows a picture twice now and then in its heavy parts, with the decoder already on its own thread, and the log has `[codec] AV1 decoder 1: the decoder is behind the movie: of the last 600 packets decoded in … ms each, … ms the longest; … pictures came after their buffer was released`.
- **Cause:** dav1d was opened with two task threads and `max_frame_delay` 1: one frame at a time, so that a temporal unit's picture was there when its send returned. A hierarchical stream puts several frames in one temporal unit (the hidden ones the next units only show): Ocarina of Time's title movie took 80 to 122 ms for one unit in about 32 on an M2 Pro, and 15.4 ms a unit over its heaviest five seconds, at a budget of 16.7 ms. More task threads alone gave nothing (4 threads on one frame: the same times).
- **Fix:** dav1d decodes on 4 threads, 3 frames at once (`DAV1D_THREADS`, `DAV1D_FRAMES` in `hle_movie_av1.c`): 7.1 ms a unit over the same five seconds and 42 to 76 ms the longest (2 frames at once 8.8 ms; 6 threads on 3 frames no better). Its pictures now come out later than their units go in, so each unit's time rides with it (`Dav1dData.m.timestamp`, `mav1_picture.ts`) and the decoder's thread asks for more pictures after a unit (`mav1_next`). When no packet waits behind a unit, and at the end of the stream, it drains dav1d, so a picture is not kept back for a packet that may not come.
- **A drain goes to its end:** a `dav1d_get_picture` with no data sent before it is dav1d's drain (`drain_picture`, `lib.c`, 1.5.4), which steps over its empty frame contexts on the way to the oldest frame. Data sent between two such calls leaves `frame_thread.next` on a frame still in flight; that frame is then retired with `task_thread.first` one context past the oldest for good, and retiring a frame cancels the task rescan asked for `task_thread.first` (`reset_task_cur`), now a live frame's. Its task is never run: the workers sleep in `dav1d_worker_task` and `dav1d_submit_frame` waits for ever. With a drain that stopped as soon as a packet arrived, the title movie froze for good in 3 runs of 6 (`sample`: `dm codec video` in `dav1d_submit_frame`, the four workers parked). A drain that has given a picture is now called until it gives none (`mav1_next`'s contract; `decode_dav1d` ends one left open, with a red line).
- **Check:** `t_codec_video` sends AV1 key frames through the protocol and gets each with its own time, in order, the last with the end of stream; it drains mid-stream, and runs 600 rounds of units with drains at random places.
- **Found on:** [The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/README.md): eight runs of the title on an M2 Pro (54 s each, headless, the player's settings), 0 or 1 failed `advance` in 2,567 to 3,036 a run (none in six of them) and no frozen movie; before both fixes, 546 and 569 failed. In a window, over 88 s of the title movie: 5,273 packets decoded in 4.82 ms each, 87 ms the longest, 2 pictures after their buffer was released (the totals line at Finalize).
- **The log line:** the decoder's thread counts the pictures that found a released buffer already waiting for them (the client had run out of decoded pictures) and times each packet (`cv_stat`). A run of 600 packets with more such pictures than the decoder has buffers prints the line above; Finalize prints the totals. On the title movie: 0 to 3 in a run with frame threads, 29 and 18 in the two heaviest without, where `advance` failed 5 to 7 times.
- **Open:** measured on an M2 Pro only (8 performance cores). An M1 or M2 with 4 performance cores was not run; there the log line says whether the decoder keeps up.

### VideoToolbox session invalidation consumes the rest of a movie

- **Symptom:** where VideoToolbox decodes AV1 and its session is invalidated during a movie (found on an iPhone after a background/foreground round trip), `[RED] movie: AV1 decode submission failed (-12903, first case)` appears and the movie ends within a few seconds, with many fewer pictures than input packets.
- **Cause:** `kVTInvalidSessionErr` means the session no longer owns usable decoder state. The host kept submitting to that session: it was replaced only when the sequence header changed. Each failed packet was consumed without an output picture, so output-buffer backpressure no longer bounded the player. A replacement session also needs the reference pictures established by earlier compressed packets; merely opening it on an inter frame cannot reconstruct those references. On the affected iPhone, one inactive-only transition succeeded; a later full background/foreground transition invalidated the session. That trace establishes the host's missing recovery, not Apple's internal reason for revoking the decoder.
- **Fix:** `hle_movie_av1.c` retains the compressed samples since the last key frame. An invalid-session status from either submission or the synchronous output callback creates a replacement and replays that history with `kVTDecodeFrame_DoNotOutputFrame`, then decodes the interrupted packet. Replay restores VideoToolbox's private references without modifying the host's presentation-order queue or emitting pictures twice. Every original picture keeps its timestamp; no pictures are skipped or replaced by stills, and there is no profile/environment switch. Sequence changes, a new key frame and `Flush` discard the old history. Stop, Flush and Finalize cancel the session being made and the replay (`movie_vt_cancelled`, `hle_movie_vt.h`), so a stopped decoder cannot deadlock or deliver the old packet after a seek. nxnative also held session creation and replay back while the application was not active, for iOS; the Mac host never leaves the active state, and that wait is gone.
- **Cost:** compressed history and one retain/release per sample, rather than an extra uncompressed picture. History size and recovery work depend on the stream's key-frame interval. Ocarina's 9,254-picture opening has 29 key frames, at most 5.35 seconds apart; its largest history contains 13,282,833 compressed bytes (12.7 MiB), plus CoreMedia metadata. Re-decoding the preceding interval costs CPU and decoder work only on session invalidation; no gameplay performance comparison has been measured.
- **Check:** where the Mac has a hardware AV1 decoder, `t_codec_video` invalidates a real AV1 session after a picture: without the recovery 1/3 pictures come back, with it 3/3. Its synthetic inter-frame movies compare every picture's pixel hash and timestamp against uninterrupted decoding, over three loops with timestamps restarting, with invalidation at each packet position and a second invalidation during replay or resubmission: every original picture matches, AV1 show-existing-frame units included. A hidden AV1 key frame does not discard older references. The threaded `nn::codec` cases inject a callback error and hold the replacement session while it is being made, then check its completion and its cancellation by Flush, Stop and Finalize. Before the H.264 cases were removed with that decoder, the test passed under ASan/UBSan on an Apple M4 Pro (macOS 27.2.1) and on the affected iPhone. Open: the cases were not run since on a Mac with a hardware AV1 decoder (the M2 Pro they were rewritten on skips them).
- **Found on:** [The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/README.md), [PR #2877](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/2877), reported by kencalx and ported by Oliver Ames. A fresh run on iPhone 18 Pro Max, iOS 27.2 (24B119), installed build `34ada0283e7d`, reproduced `-12903` immediately after background/foreground, followed about two seconds later by end of stream after 840 pictures and 9,255 decoded packets. The PR's earlier account says 365/9,254 pictures; the initial review request said 1,365, and that earlier count has no accompanying raw log here.

### Movie extractor allocations need the SDK graphics allocator

- **Symptom:** `CRASH sig=11 addr=0 pc=sdk+0x40ca1c lr=subsdk0+0x32455c` after `StoryEndOfMovieCallback`, while opening another MP4.
- **Cause:** the host took over `nv::SetGraphicsAllocator` and `nv::SetGraphicsDevtoolsAllocator` and only recorded the callbacks, which nothing in the host read. The SDK's setter is what installs the dispatch behind `NvOsAlloc`, `NvOsAllocAlign`, `NvOsRealloc` and `NvOsFree`, and the movie extractor still allocates through them: `MPEG4Extractor::parseChunk` allocates a 224-byte `SampleTable` through `android::HeapObject::operator new` → `NvOsAlloc`. With no allocator installed it used the SDK's 32 KB bootstrap heap and tried to grow it through `nn::os::AllocateMemoryBlock`, instead of the allocator the game supplied. That allocation returned null; `android::RefBase`'s constructor then stored its vtable through null.
- **Fix:** [`dm/gfx/hle_nv.c`](../../dm/gfx/hle_nv.c) no longer takes over either setter: the game's call reaches the SDK's own, as on the console. In the sdk modules checked (3.7.0, 11.4.0, 14.3.0, and the Switch 2 SDKs 20.5.13, 20.5.29 and 21.4.3) it checks its arguments, aborts when called twice or after `nv::InitializeGraphics` (whose flag the host's take-over of that function never sets), and installs the NvOs dispatch; on Switch 2 it also calls `nn::gs::sdk::InitializeGraphicsAllocator`. Driver initialization and heap sizes are unchanged.
- **Check:** Mega Man Legacy Collection (SDK 3.7.0) and Fast Fusion (Switch 2, SDK 20.5.13), whose `main` calls the setter, reach their frame loops through the SDK's own setter with the same log as before (Mac, 2026-11-16). Mario Golf: Super Rush, which never calls it, plays its VP9 opening movie as on main. Mortal Kombat 11's story transition was not rerun on this exact change; its contributor reported it working with the SDK setter called (2026-11-15).
- **Found on:** Mortal Kombat 11 (`0100F2200C984000`), 1.0.26, SDK 9 (`sdk` build `d145f1048fc57e03685da0441d823ce4`).

## Movie players

### A player limited to one picture a frame falls behind its sound

- **Symptom:** a movie's sound runs ahead of its picture and ends before it, so the movie's later scenes play in silence: the log's `audren: renderer 0 rendered N frames, M of them not silent` shows the mix silent for up to a minute or more, with no underrun and no red line, and the movie's decoder reaches `end of stream` long after the movie's length. Seen where frames take two vsyncs during a 60-picture movie, which [the frame step's movie rate](frame-rate.md#the-frame-step-follows-a-movies-picture-rate) cannot prevent: a GPU that does not hold 60 there (a slower or a busy Mac) and the thermal cap, which holds movies at 30; before that rule, every time with the frame rate set to 30. The game's speed reads right.
- **Cause:** the game's player, not the decoder. Ocarina of Time's (in `main`, v131072) adds each frame's time to a count of pictures due (`main+0x3b5ca60`: the time object's frame time × 60, at the movie's rate) and advances that many (`main+0x3b61ea0` pops them from its queue and shows the last), but at most the limit its constructor set (`main+0x3b5cb2c`); what is left stays in the count. The constructor (`main+0x3b5cec0`) sets the limit to 1, or to 2 for a last argument of 2 or more, and the queue to twice the limit; the game's one caller passes 1. The console holds one vsync a frame in its movies. Here the [frame step](frame-rate.md#a-fixed-frame-step-plays-in-slow-motion-on-a-slow-mac) hands the game steps of two and three vsyncs: the count asks for two pictures, one is taken, and the picture is behind for the rest of the movie, while the movie's sound, a stream of the game's sound engine, keeps real time. Read in play (`DM_PROBE=main:3b5cb28` with `DM_PROBE_ALL=1`: `x8` the pictures due, `x9` the limit): the limit 1 and the count at 1,350 pictures after 45 s at a 30 fps frame step.
- **Fix:** a function-matched cut in the profile ([`patches.py`](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/patches.py), `MATCH_CUTS`): the constructor takes its own value for 2 whatever its caller passes (limit 2, queue 4), so a frame of two vsyncs shows every second picture and a movie that fell behind catches up at two pictures a frame. It needs [the movie's frame to count pictures](#a-movies-frame-counts-frames-drawn-not-pictures), or the event's subtitles, voice and logo fall behind the picture instead. It is a change of the game's code because the limit is the game's: the host's decoder gives every picture it is asked for. A second cut lets a frame of three vsyncs, the frame step's largest hold, take three: the update's cap, its one read of the limit (`ldr w9, [x0, #0x20]` at `main+0x3b5cb20`), becomes 3. 3 cannot be the limit itself, because the limit also sizes the decoder's pipeline, which has slots for 2: a picture pool of twice the limit, registered from a four-slot array at the player's `+0x168`, and as many inputs in flight as the limit, in two buffer slots at `+0x188` and `+0x190` (`main+0x3b5ff30` takes slot 0 for an id of 2 or more). With the game's pool of 6 the registration wrote over the input buffers (the game stopped at the movie's start with a copy to a null pointer in `sdk`). With the pool kept at 4, a third input reused slot 0 while it was in flight (a VideoToolbox decode error after 150 pictures of the title movie). The advance pops up to the cap from its own queue and stops when the queue is empty, so a third picture is taken only when the decoder has one.
- **Check:** a host with one log line per 60 pictures (stream time against host time) and per 200 renderer frames (how many not silent), headless, `DM_FPS_TARGET=30`, `DM_KEYS=50:A~0.2` from an empty save (the title, then the opening's first movie: 9,254 pictures at 60 a second, 154.2 s).
- **Found on:** [The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/README.md#frame-rate) ([issue 1501](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/1501)), numbers in its note.
- **Open:** frames of four vsyncs or more (loading hitches) still leave the picture behind until frames of one or two vsyncs catch it up (they take up to 3 where 1 or 2 are due). No other game was found with this player: the constructor's fingerprint matches no function in the `main`s of nine other Nintendo games on hand, and Breath of the Wild's and Tears of the Kingdom's movies go through `movie::Decoder` instead.

### A movie's frame counts frames drawn, not pictures

- **Symptom:** a movie's subtitles, its voice lines and the overlays its event shows (Ocarina of Time's title logo) fall behind its picture and sound, about twice as late when frames take two vsyncs; one due after the movie's last picture never shows. No red line. Seen only once the player takes more than one picture a frame ([A player limited to one picture a frame falls behind its sound](#a-player-limited-to-one-picture-a-frame-falls-behind-its-sound)); before that, the picture was as late as they were.
- **Cause:** the game's event follows the movie's frame, not the movie's time. Ocarina of Time's movie event (its update `main+0x3b3e9a0`, v131072) reads the movie's frame through its vtable's `+0x78` (`main+0x3b3ec10`: the movie object's int at `+0x2f8`), moves its timeline up to it when the timeline is behind (`main+0x122d520`), and measures the movie's sound stream against it (`main+0x3b3ea50`). The timeline carries the subtitles, the narrator's voice lines (`EventFlowMsg/Dm_Kokiri_0000`), rumble and the logo (`EventOpenOpeningTitle`, in `Event/EventFlow/Dm_Kokiri_0000.bfevfl`). The movie's draw (`main+0x3b5c710`, and `main+0x3b5c870` for its first draw) adds 1 to that frame on each frame a picture is on screen. That equals the pictures shown only while the player takes one a frame.
- **Fix:** a function-matched cut in the profile ([`patches.py`](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/patches.py), `MATCH_CUTS`, one patch of both draws): the frame becomes the player's own count of pictures taken (the player's `+0xc`, which its update adds to and its start clears, as the movie's start clears `+0x2f8`). It is the game's code because the counter is the game's.
- **Check:** frames forced to two vsyncs (`DM_TEST_PRESENT_LAG=1,20`) from an empty save into the opening, a shot every 4 s: with both cuts every subtitle and the logo on the picture they have at 60 fps, the movie through all 9,254 pictures; with the picture cut alone the subtitles at about twice their times and the logo missing.
- **Found on:** [The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time](../../dm/games/0400486024DEC000/README.md#frame-rate) ([issue 1501](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/1501), [PR #2072](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/2072)), shot times in its note.
